## Page 2 - How We Choose The Right AI Use Case

Good AI automation starts with the right use case, not with a model.

Aureus looks for processes with five signals:

- **repeated work** - the task happens often enough to justify a system,
- **skill bottleneck** - people spend expert time on sorting, checking, or rewriting,
- **unclear ownership / sensitive decision** - the next step matters and needs review,
- **evidence-ready process** - the workflow can keep a visible record,
- **bounded pilot** - the first version can be tested safely before scale.

**Discovery flow**

```text
Discover -> Score -> Design -> Build -> Review -> Scale
```

The goal is not to automate everything. The goal is to find the first controlled workflow that creates visible value and can be reviewed.

## Page 3 - Automation Audit

**One-line promise:** Find the first useful AI automation before building anything expensive.

**Buyer problem**

The company knows work is manual and messy, but the first useful automation is unclear. People have ideas, tools, and pain points, but no safe build order.

**What AI prepares**

AI helps summarize process notes, cluster repeated work, identify bottlenecks, draft candidate workflows, and prepare an impact / effort map.

**What people approve**

The owner confirms which process matters, which actions are sensitive, what must stay manual, and what a safe first pilot can include.

**What evidence remains**

Process map, automation candidate list, review-boundary table, risk list, first pilot brief, and next-step recommendation.

**Controlled workflow**

```text
intake -> process map -> candidate list -> impact/effort score -> review boundary -> pilot brief
```

**Client receives**

- a clear process map,
- ranked automation candidates,
- review and risk boundaries,
- first pilot recommendation,
- a decision on what to build next.

**Proof status:** Public-safe concept; recommended first purchase; pilot-ready.

**Boundaries**

No claim that every process should be automated. No promised savings. No production deployment claim from the audit alone.

**Best first step**

Send one repeated process that wastes time or creates avoidable mistakes.

## Page 4 - n8n Workflow Review + Build

**One-line promise:** Turn fragile automations into reviewable systems people can operate.

**Buyer problem**

A workflow may run, but the team cannot explain failure paths, ownership, credentials, retries, live actions, or handoff. That makes the automation hard to trust.

**What AI prepares**

AI helps inspect workflow intent, summarize logic, draft documentation, identify weak boundaries, prepare validation checklists, and propose safer structure.

**What people approve**

The owner approves credential handling, live activation, external sends, production changes, retry behavior, and what happens when the workflow fails.

**What evidence remains**

Risk scan, workflow map, failure-path notes, validation checklist, approval boundary, and handoff note.

**Controlled workflow**

```text
trigger -> input contract -> AI-assisted step -> validation -> approval -> evidence note -> handoff
```

**Client receives**

- workflow review notes,
- failure and ownership map,
- validation plan,
- safe build or repair direction,
- handoff documentation.

**Proof status:** Public-safe concept; setup-gated; pilot-ready.

**Boundaries**

No live activation, credential change, external send, or production action without explicit approval.

**Best first step**

Share a sanitized workflow description and the failure you are most worried about.

**Git-backed truth notes**

Mapped to the public portfolio offer menu, n8n governance approach, validation scripts, and workflow proof-pack discipline.

## Page 5 - FinEcon Pocket / Bridge

**One-line promise:** Move documents from intake to reviewed handoff with proof, while keeping accountant validation in place.

**Buyer problem**

Invoices, receipts, and documents arrive through different channels. Context gets lost, review is unclear, and the handoff into accounting systems becomes hard to inspect.

**What AI prepares**

AI may extract candidate fields, classify document type, summarize context, flag missing information, draft review notes, and prepare downstream handoff data.

**What people approve**

Humans review uncertain fields, accounting-sensitive interpretation, exceptions, bridge readiness, downstream handoff, and anything that affects official records.

**What evidence remains**

Document status, review decision, bridge readiness note, proof pack, exception list, writeback direction, and accountant review checklist.

**Controlled workflow**

```text
Pocket intake / prepared folder
-> status tracking
-> review action
-> bridge start
-> POHODA preflight
-> reviewed handoff
-> post-import writeback
-> proof pack
-> accountant validation boundary
```

**Client receives**

- document intake path,
- review queue direction,
- POHODA handoff model,
- proof pack direction,
- exception list,
- accountant review checklist.

**Proof status:** Internal E2E passed; accountant validation pending; setup-gated; pilot-ready.

**Source-backed product facts**

The source-backed FinEcon stack includes 19 workflow exports. The Pocket layer includes:

- FinEcon 13 - Pocket Document Intake,
- FinEcon 14 - Pocket Status API,
- FinEcon 15 - Pocket Review Action,
- FinEcon 16 - Pocket Bridge Start,
- FinEcon 17 - Pocket Company Registration.

The Bridge layer includes:

- FinEcon 09 - Bridge Review to POHODA,
- FinEcon 10 - Bridge Preflight & Runtime Readiness,
- FinEcon 11 - Bridge Live Import,
- FinEcon 12 - Bridge Post-Import Writeback & Proof Pack.

The proof layer includes:

- FinEcon 19 - Proof Pack Drive Publisher.

Internal status evidence says the Core E2E passed, FinEcon Pocket to POHODA passed, and POHODA live import passed across three configured mServer environments. Accounting correctness and cleanup remain pending accountant validation.

**Boundaries**

FinEcon is not an accounting authority. It does not replace accountants, provide tax/legal advice, or claim accounting correctness before professional validation.

**Best first step**

Choose one document flow and define what a person or accountant must approve before downstream use.

## Page 6 - Approval-Safe Sales Machine

**One-line promise:** Use AI to prepare sales work without letting it send blind outreach.

**Buyer problem**

Leads get researched once, then follow-up depends on memory. Messages are inconsistent, claims are risky, and no one knows which lead needs review.

**What AI prepares**

AI may research public context, classify lead fit, draft outreach, draft follow-up, classify replies, and prepare booking or next-step notes.

**What people approve**

Humans approve claims, external messages, sensitive personalization, do-not-contact decisions, and any send action.

**What evidence remains**

Lead state, qualification note, draft message, approval status, reply classification, follow-up plan, and daily report.

**Controlled workflow**

```text
lead source -> discovery -> qualification -> draft outreach -> manual approval -> follow-up draft -> reply classification -> booking draft -> daily report -> audit log
```

**Client receives**

- safe lead state model,
- approved-message workflow,
- reply handling direction,
- do-not-contact boundary,
- reporting structure.

**Proof status:** Public-safe concept; setup-gated; pilot-ready.

**Boundaries**

No blind outreach. No sending without approval. No unsupported claim generation.

**Best first step**

Start with one lead source and one approved offer message.

**Git-backed truth notes**

Mapped to the public Sales Machine proof package, safe state model, workflow map, and buyer example.


## Page 10 - 30-Day Client Pilot Path

The goal is not to automate everything in 30 days. The goal is to prove one controlled workflow that the client understands, approves, and can inspect.

| Week | Focus | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Discovery and process map | The real process, owners, inputs, exceptions, and risk points are visible. |
| Week 2 | Pilot design and review boundary | The safe pilot scope, AI role, approval points, and acceptance criteria are defined. |
| Week 3 | Build / test controlled proof | A controlled workflow or proof surface is tested with synthetic or approved examples. |
| Week 4 | Review, handoff, next decision | The client receives evidence, risk notes, handoff, and a clear decision on the next phase. |

**By day 30 the client receives**

- process map,
- pilot spec,
- review boundary,
- evidence example,
- risk list,
- next decision.
